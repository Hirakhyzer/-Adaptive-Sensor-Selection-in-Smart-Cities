# Governance and Ethics

The **Adaptive Sensor Selection in Smart Cities** repository is an independent synthetic research prototype for studying dynamic sensor group selection in smart-city AIoT environments.

## 1. Intended use

Acceptable uses include:

- Studying adaptive sensor selection with fictional smart-city scenarios.
- Teaching AIoT, smart infrastructure, traffic analytics, and energy-aware sensing.
- Comparing sensor-group policies for energy, latency, visibility, and safety trade-offs.
- Prototyping transparent decision-support workflows before using real sensor data.
- Building reproducible experiments and audit trails for academic research.

## 2. Non-intended use

The project is not intended for:

- Live city infrastructure control.
- Traffic-signal control or emergency response automation.
- Surveillance, policing, or public safety enforcement.
- Certified road-safety, transport, environmental, or legal compliance claims.
- Deployment to real sensors without hardware validation and governance.
- Automated decisions affecting citizens, drivers, pedestrians, or communities.

## 3. Synthetic-data boundary

The dataset and examples are synthetic. They may represent traffic density, weather, visibility, energy consumption, latency, and sensor activation patterns, but they are not measured city data and should not be presented as real-world performance.

## 4. Privacy principle

Real smart-city sensors can capture sensitive location, movement, vehicle, pedestrian, and behavioral information. Real deployments require data minimization, aggregation, retention limits, privacy impact assessment, cybersecurity safeguards, and public transparency.

## 5. Safety principle

Sensor selection can affect road monitoring, hazard detection, traffic-management decisions, and emergency response awareness. Any deployment-oriented work requires fail-safe defaults, human oversight, hardware validation, incident review, and safety engineering.

## 6. Equity principle

Smart-city systems can distribute monitoring quality unevenly across neighborhoods. Any real-world study should evaluate whether energy-saving policies reduce service quality for specific areas, communities, road types, or mobility groups.

## 7. Responsible statement

This lab supports learning, simulation, and research. It should never be used as the sole basis for real infrastructure control, surveillance, traffic enforcement, emergency routing, public warnings, or official city-policy decisions.
